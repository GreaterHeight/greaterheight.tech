System Architecture

Five layers from physical sensor to business decision

Every IoT system we build is designed across all five layers from the outset. Attempting to design one layer independently of the others is what produces systems that work in a proof-of-concept and fail under production conditions.

01
Perception Layer
The physical instrumentation: sensors and devices that measure or detect conditions in the real world. Sensor selection, environmental hardening for field conditions, power management (mains, solar, battery), and device provisioning strategy all sit at this layer. Choosing the wrong sensor for an environment — or the wrong mounting approach — means the data is wrong from the start regardless of how good the rest of the stack is.
Temperature & humidity sensorsFlow metersPressure transducersGPS trackersLevel sensorsVibration monitorsCCTV camerasFuel probes
02
Connectivity Layer
How device data travels from the field to processing infrastructure. In Nigeria and many African markets, connectivity is not a commodity — it is a design variable. We evaluate GSM/4G, LPWAN (LoRaWAN, NB-IoT), satellite, and local radio options against coverage, bandwidth, latency, power consumption, and cost per device before making protocol decisions. Gateway design, message queuing, and connection resilience under intermittent signal are designed at this layer.
MQTTCoAPLoRaWANNB-IoT4G/LTE cellularSatellite uplinkIndustrial gateways
03
Edge Computing Layer
Local processing that happens before data reaches the cloud or central platform. In environments where connectivity is unreliable or where response time under 500ms is required — such as equipment protection shutdowns — waiting for a cloud round-trip is not viable. Edge devices run filtering, normalisation, threshold alerting, and local storage for resilience. Edge processing also reduces data volumes, lowering cloud ingestion cost and connectivity bandwidth requirements.
Edge gatewaysRaspberry Pi / industrial SBCsOPC-UA bridgingLocal alert logicStore-and-forward
04
Platform Layer
The cloud or on-premise backend that ingests, stores, processes, and exposes device data at scale. This includes the time-series database, stream processing pipelines for real-time analytics, batch processing for historical analysis, the data retention and archiving strategy, and the APIs through which application-layer systems consume the data. We design this layer to handle both the volume of data the current deployment generates and the growth the roadmap projects.
AWS IoT Core / Azure IoT HubInfluxDB / TimescaleDBApache KafkaREST & WebSocket APIsData warehousing
05
Application Layer
The interfaces and systems through which humans and business processes interact with the IoT data. Operational dashboards showing current equipment status and alarms, analytics reports for maintenance planning, threshold-triggered alerts delivered to mobile devices or email, integrations with ERP and SCADA systems, and the automated business rules that trigger actions based on sensor readings. This is the layer that determines whether the IoT investment changes how the organisation operates.
Operational dashboardsMobile alertsERP integrationSCADA bridgeMaintenance schedulingManagement reports
Industry Applications

Where IoT creates measurable operational value in Nigerian enterprises

IoT technology is not industry-neutral. The sensor types, connectivity requirements, latency tolerance, and integration demands vary substantially between sectors. These are the industries where we have deployed or are actively building IoT systems.

Oil, Gas & Energy

Equipment monitoring and production intelligence across upstream, midstream, and downstream operations

Remote monitoring of wellheads, flow stations, pipelines, and storage tanks eliminates the guesswork that leads to unplanned shutdowns and costly maintenance dispatches. Real-time production data surfaces underperforming wells and anomalies before they become critical incidents.

Tank level and flow monitoring with automated custody transfer logging
Generator fuel consumption tracking and remote start/stop management
Pipeline pressure and leak detection monitoring
Production well performance dashboards updated every minute
Manufacturing & Industry

Machine health monitoring and production line intelligence that reduces downtime and waste

Unplanned machine downtime is one of the highest-cost events in manufacturing. Vibration, temperature, and power consumption data from production equipment, analysed over time, makes the difference between reactive repairs and scheduled preventive maintenance at a fraction of the cost.

Predictive maintenance alerts based on vibration and temperature trend analysis
OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness) measurement and reporting
Energy consumption monitoring per machine and production line
Quality control integration: sensor data correlated with product defect rates
Healthcare

Medical equipment monitoring, cold chain compliance, and facility environment management

Healthcare IoT in Nigeria addresses the specific operational risks of the sector: pharmaceutical cold chain compliance under power instability, medical equipment availability, and the gap between what the equipment log says and what actually happened. Automated monitoring removes that gap.

Pharmacy cold storage temperature monitoring with power-failure alerting
Medical gas pressure monitoring and inventory management
Generator uptime tracking for facilities reliant on backup power
Laboratory sample temperature monitoring and audit trail
Logistics & Supply Chain

Fleet tracking, cold chain compliance, and warehouse environment monitoring for goods in transit

Cargo condition and vehicle location are only useful data points if they are real-time and verifiable. IoT enables logistics operators to offer customers documented proof that temperature-sensitive cargo was handled within specification throughout the journey, and to identify route and driver inefficiencies before they erode margins.

Fleet GPS tracking with geofencing and route deviation alerts
Refrigerated vehicle temperature monitoring with continuous logging
Warehouse cold room monitoring with automatic customer notification
Fuel monitoring and driver behaviour analytics
What We Build

The specific systems our IoT engagements deliver

IoT is an umbrella term. These are the specific system types we design and build, described precisely enough to judge whether they match what you are trying to accomplish.

Remote Monitoring Systems

End-to-end systems that connect field sensors to a central dashboard, with alerting, historical data storage, and reporting. Designed around your specific assets, communication environment, and the operational decisions the data should support.

Predictive Maintenance Platforms

Continuous monitoring of equipment health indicators — vibration, temperature, acoustic emission, and power draw — combined with analytics that surface degradation trends before they become failures. Maintenance alerts delivered to technicians before the equipment stops.

Fleet & Asset Tracking

Vehicle location tracking, journey logging, geofencing, and driver behaviour monitoring. Integrated with fuel consumption sensors where required. Built to work across Nigeria's varied GSM coverage landscape, with local data buffering for areas of poor signal.

Environmental & Compliance Monitoring

Temperature, humidity, and air quality monitoring for facilities where environmental conditions are either regulated or operationally critical. Automatic alerting on breach, continuous logging for audit purposes, and integration with facility management workflows.

Energy & Utility Monitoring

Sub-metering of electricity, water, and fuel consumption at facility, line, and machine level. Identification of waste and anomalies, benchmarking against targets, and dashboards that give facility managers the data to reduce utility costs with specific interventions rather than general appeals.

ERP & SCADA Integration

Bridging IoT platform data into existing enterprise systems so that sensor readings drive business processes — triggering maintenance work orders, updating inventory systems as levels fall, or feeding production data into ERP reporting — rather than sitting in a separate IoT dashboard that nobody opens.

Why IoT Projects Fail

The problems are almost never the hardware

Most IoT projects that underperform do so not because the sensors failed or the connectivity was insufficient, but because the system was designed around what the technology could do rather than around what the organisation needs to decide. The hardware works. The data flows. Nobody looks at the dashboard because it does not tell them anything they can act on.

No defined use case before procurement

When a system is built before the specific decisions it should support are defined, it generates data volumes nobody has time to interpret and dashboards that measure things no one has authority to change.

Underestimated connectivity requirements

Proof-of-concept deployments in well-serviced urban locations then rolled out to remote or industrial sites where GSM coverage is intermittent. Data loss in the connectivity layer means the monitoring system has gaps exactly where the conditions are most severe.

No integration with existing workflows

IoT alerts that require a technician to log into a separate platform to see them, rather than arriving in the WhatsApp group or the ERP system where the team already operates, are routinely missed.

Who We Work With

Organisations ready to instrument their physical operations

Industrial and Manufacturing Operators

Organisations with significant capital equipment whose performance, availability, and operating condition determines production output. They have been managing equipment reactively and want to move to data-driven maintenance and performance optimisation.

Regulated Businesses with Compliance Requirements

Pharmaceutical distributors, food and beverage manufacturers, healthcare facilities, and financial institutions that must demonstrate continuous compliance with temperature, environmental, or security conditions and produce auditable records to regulators or customers.

Logistics and Supply Chain Operators

Haulage, cold chain, and third-party logistics companies that need to give customers verified, documented proof of condition for cargo in transit, and that want to reduce fuel cost and improve fleet utilisation through accurate data on vehicle usage.

Organisations with Distributed Remote Assets

Energy companies, water utilities, telecoms tower operators, and similar organisations managing assets spread across multiple locations who cannot economically station personnel at every site but need visibility into asset status and performance from a central location.
