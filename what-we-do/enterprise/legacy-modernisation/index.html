What Legacy Systems Cost You

Six ways your legacy system is working against you

These are not future risks. They are present costs that show up in your operations, your budget, and your team's morale every single day.

Unplanned Downtime

Outdated infrastructure fails without warning. Each outage disrupts revenue, erodes client confidence, and costs your team hours of unplanned crisis management.

Security Vulnerabilities

Unmaintained systems accumulate known exploits. Without vendor support or security patches, a single breach can expose your customers, your data, and your regulatory standing.

Slow Time to Market

Monolithic architectures make even small changes months-long projects. Competitors with modern systems ship features in days while your team is blocked by dependencies.

Escalating Maintenance Costs

The older the system, the more expensive it is to keep running. Specialist skills for obsolete technologies command premium rates, and the cost of that expertise rises every year.

Talent Attrition

Skilled engineers do not want to work in outdated environments. Legacy systems make it difficult to attract and retain the people who could help you evolve.

Compliance Risk

Regulators do not accept "our system cannot support it" as an answer. As compliance requirements evolve, legacy platforms increasingly put you at risk of fines and sanctions.

Modernisation Strategies

Four paths to modernisation. We recommend the one that fits your situation.

Not every legacy system needs to be rebuilt from scratch. The right approach depends on the age of the system, the business risk of change, and the value of what already exists.

Path 01

Rehost

Lift and shift to modern infrastructure

Move the existing application to cloud or modern on-premise infrastructure with minimal code changes. The system behaves the same but runs on stable, maintainable infrastructure.

Best when

Speed of migration matters most, the application logic is sound, and the primary concern is infrastructure stability or cost reduction.

Path 02

Replatform

Move to a better runtime with targeted improvements

Migrate to a new platform while making selective optimisations: upgrading the database engine, adopting managed services, or introducing containerisation without rewiring the core logic.

Best when

The application architecture is acceptable but the runtime environment is obsolete, and you want efficiency gains without a full rewrite.

Path 03

Rearchitect

Decompose the monolith into scalable services

Break the monolithic application into well-defined services or microservices. Each component can be updated, scaled, and deployed independently, eliminating the cascade failure risk of a tightly coupled system.

Best when

The application has years of life remaining but its architecture prevents scaling, and the business needs independent team ownership of components.

Path 04

Replace

Build the successor system alongside the current one

When the existing system is beyond rehabilitation, we build its replacement in parallel. The two systems run side by side during migration, with users moved in cohorts rather than through a single high-risk cutover.

Best when

The technical debt in the current system outweighs the value of preserving any part of it, or the business requirements have changed fundamentally since it was built.

Our Process

From assessment to handover in five governed phases

Each phase has defined gates, deliverables, and sign-off criteria. Nothing moves forward until the current phase is validated.

Phase 01

Discovery & Assessment

Map the current system, identify risks, document dependencies, and assess compliance requirements.

Weeks 1–3
Phase 02

Strategy & Roadmap

Select the modernisation path, define the target architecture, and produce a phased migration plan.

Weeks 4–5
Phase 03

Build & Migrate

Develop the new components or infrastructure in parallel. Migrate data with rollback capability at each stage.

Weeks 6–18+
Phase 04

Validation & Testing

Stress test the new system against production-equivalent loads. Confirm functional and non-functional requirements are met.

Weeks 16–20+
Phase 05

Transition & Stabilise

Go live with controlled cutover, monitor intensively, and hand over to your internal team with full documentation.

Weeks 20–24+
