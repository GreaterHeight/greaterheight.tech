Product Engineering — SaaS

Build a product that grows
its own revenue

SaaS is not a technology decision — it is a business model decision. The architecture, the billing layer, the onboarding flow, and the usage instrumentation all need to be designed with recurring revenue in mind from the first sprint. We build SaaS products that are structured to retain customers, reduce churn, and scale monthly recurring revenue over time.

What We Build

The six technical layers of a production SaaS product

A SaaS product is not a web application with a subscription. Each of these layers is a distinct engineering domain that determines whether the product can retain customers at scale.

🏠
Multi-tenant Architecture
Row-level or schema-level tenant isolation. Every customer's data is logically separated. Access control is enforced at the database layer, not just the application layer. Organisations, teams, and individual users modelled as a hierarchy.
💳
Subscription Billing
Stripe or Paystack integration. Subscription creation, plan changes, proration, failed payment recovery, invoice generation, free trials, usage-based billing, and annual billing discount logic. No bespoke billing reinvention.
📈
Onboarding and Activation
The guided setup sequence from account creation to first value moment. Empty states, contextual help, in-app tooltips, activation email sequences, and the instrumentation to know whether users are completing onboarding.
📊
Usage Analytics and Instrumentation
Event tracking on the behaviours that predict conversion, retention, and expansion. Feature adoption heatmaps, cohort retention reports, and admin dashboards that make product decisions data-driven rather than opinion-driven.
🔓
Role-Based Access Control
Owners, admins, members, and viewer roles within each tenant. Permission checks enforced at the API layer. Audit logs for sensitive actions. SSO and directory sync for enterprise plans, where the product needs it.
📣
Notifications and Engagement
In-app notification centre, transactional email via SendGrid or Postmark, SMS or WhatsApp alerts for critical events, and usage-triggered engagement sequences. Configured per-tenant per preference, not system-wide.
Architecture Decisions

The choices that cannot be undone cheaply

Some architectural decisions in a SaaS product are reversible with effort. Others are structural — reversing them means rebuilding from scratch. We make these decisions deliberately at the start, not reactively once the product has customers.

01

Tenant isolation model

Row-level, schema-level, or database-per-tenant. Each has cost, compliance, and scalability trade-offs. We recommend based on the target market and customer base.

02

Subscription data model

How plans, features, limits, and usage caps are stored and enforced. The schema here drives every billing and access-control decision for the life of the product.

03

Soft-delete versus hard-delete

Cancelled accounts, removed members, and deleted workspaces. SaaS products almost always need soft-delete with restoration paths for churned customers.

04

API versioning from the start

If the product will ever have customers who integrate with it, API versioning must be designed before the first external customer, not retrofitted after.

Growth Architecture

The product growth loop — built in, not bolted on

The SaaS products that grow their own revenue have a flywheel — a cycle of acquisition, activation, retention, and expansion that reinforces itself. We engineer that loop into the product from the beginning.

Stage 1

Acquisition

Self-serve sign-up. No sales call required. The product's public landing pages and free trial convert visitors into accounts without manual intervention.

Stage 2

Activation

The onboarding flow guides new users to their first genuine value moment. The activation event is defined, instrumented, and monitored from day one.

Growth
Loop
Stage 3

Retention

Usage analytics identify at-risk accounts before they churn. Re-engagement sequences, usage alerts, and in-app success prompts reduce involuntary churn.

Stage 4

Expansion

Usage-based upgrade prompts, seat-count growth, and plan-tier upsell flows mean that growing customers grow your MRR automatically, with no sales effort.

Billing Infrastructure

Billing is not a feature. It is infrastructure.

Getting billing wrong costs you revenue in ways that are hard to attribute — failed payment recovery, proration errors on plan changes, and invoices that cannot pass a finance audit. We treat billing with the same rigour as the data model.

Stripe Paystack Flutterwave Webhooks Usage metering
🔐

Subscription lifecycle management

Trials, conversions, upgrades, downgrades, pauses, and cancellations with correct proration logic.

🔄

Failed payment recovery

Automated retry schedules, dunning emails, and account grace periods that recover revenue without manual intervention.

📋

Invoice and tax compliance

Legally compliant invoices with VAT/GST where applicable, and audit-ready billing records for every transaction.

Common Questions

What to know before the conversation

A SaaS product must solve a problem that is worth paying for repeatedly, not just once. That changes the engineering priorities significantly. Multi-tenancy means the data model must enforce isolation between customers at the schema level. Billing integration is a first-class concern from day one. Onboarding must be self-serve and must guide a new user to their first moment of genuine value as quickly as possible. Usage analytics must track the behaviours that predict conversion and retention, not just page views. These are not features added late; they are architecture decisions made early.

The right model depends on the product's customer base and compliance requirements. Shared database with row-level tenant isolation is the most cost-effective starting point for most SaaS products and scales well through hundreds of tenants. Separate schema per tenant is appropriate when customers require demonstrable data isolation, often in regulated industries. We recommend one of these two approaches based on the product's target market and advise against hybrid models, which create operational complexity without clear benefit.

We integrate with Stripe for most SaaS billing, covering subscription creation, plan changes, proration, failed payment recovery, invoice generation, and usage-based billing where required. Paystack is used for products primarily targeting Nigerian or West African customers. The billing system is one of the most consequential decisions in a SaaS build; we do not build bespoke billing logic in-house when a mature provider can handle it reliably. We spend our engineering attention on the product, not on reimplementing what Stripe already does well.

Onboarding is the period between a user creating an account and reaching their first genuine value moment. We build the account creation flow, the guided setup sequence, the empty-state design for every screen, in-app contextual help, and the activation email sequence. We also define and instrument the activation event — the specific action that tells you a user has completed onboarding — so you can measure whether the onboarding is working.

Many engagements we work on are conversions of existing software — a one-time-sale desktop application, a bespoke enterprise system, or a service-based business where the core process can be productised. We start with a product and business model audit: what the current architecture can support, what needs to change, and what the subscription pricing model will look like. The technical migration is usually straightforward; the harder work is defining the subscription tiers and what belongs in each one.
