What Is Covered

Six categories of work, all under one retainer

Support and maintenance cover different kinds of work, but they draw on the same knowledge of your product. Separating them into different vendors creates information gaps. We cover both.

📌

Bug Triage & Resolution

Reported issues are assessed, prioritised, and resolved by the same team that knows the codebase. No handoffs to teams with no context.

📈

Infrastructure Monitoring

24/7 uptime monitoring with alerting. We know when something goes wrong before your users report it, and we act before it becomes an incident.

🔨

Dependency & Security Updates

Libraries, runtimes, and frameworks that go out of date are a security liability. We track and apply updates on a defined schedule, with testing before each release.

⚡

Performance Optimisation

Slow queries, memory leaks, and resource waste accumulate over time. Planned performance reviews keep the product responsive as usage grows.

📝

Feature Maintenance

Minor feature work, UI adjustments, copy changes, and backlog items within the allocated monthly hours, managed in a prioritised queue.

📊

Monthly Reporting

A written monthly report covers uptime statistics, issues resolved, maintenance completed, and what is planned for the next period.

When Things Escalate

Critical issues have a defined escalation path, not an ad hoc scramble

The difference between an incident that takes two hours and one that takes two days is almost never technical. It is process. We define escalation before anything goes wrong, so the team knows exactly what to do when something does.

1

Alert fires

Monitoring system detects anomaly and raises an alert within 60 seconds.

2

On-call engineer acknowledged

Assigned engineer confirms acknowledgement and begins investigation within SLA window.

3

Client notified

You receive a status update within 30 minutes of the alert, confirming the team is active on the issue.

4

Senior engineer pulled in

If resolution is not in sight within the first hour, a senior engineer joins to bring additional expertise.

5

Post-incident review

Once resolved, we document root cause, contributing factors, and measures taken to prevent recurrence.

Common Questions

Before we start

Support is reactive: a user reports something, a ticket is raised, the team investigates and resolves it. Maintenance is planned: a schedule of updates, dependency upgrades, performance tuning, and backlog work that happens regardless of whether anything breaks. The two work together — a well-maintained product generates fewer support tickets, and a support team that spots recurring issues feeds them back into the maintenance backlog. GreaterHeight provides both under a single retainer, so the same team that answers your support tickets is also managing the planned maintenance cycle.

Response times are defined in the SLA for each support tier. On the Professional tier, critical issues (production system down, data loss risk) receive an initial response within two hours during business hours; on Enterprise, critical response is available within one hour, including weekends. First response means a named engineer has acknowledged the ticket and begun investigation — not an automated acknowledgement. Resolution time depends on the nature of the issue, but we commit to continuous progress updates at defined intervals until the issue is resolved.

Yes, with a structured handover process. When inheriting an existing product, we begin with a 2-to-4-week transition period: we review the codebase, existing documentation, infrastructure, and outstanding bugs; we set up our monitoring and alerting; we shadow existing support channels; and we produce a product health report that documents what we found, what risks we identified, and what our plan is for the first 90 days. We do not take on a product cold — the transition period is mandatory, because inheriting a system without understanding it means inheriting its risks without the context to manage them.

Every retainer includes ticket handling for reported issues, infrastructure monitoring with alerting, a monthly health report, and a defined allocation of maintenance hours for planned work. The allocation varies by tier: Essential covers routine support and urgent fixes; Professional adds proactive monitoring, a larger maintenance allocation, and regular strategy calls; Enterprise adds dedicated engineering capacity, SLA guarantees across all priority levels, and direct access to a named account engineer. Each retainer is scoped during a brief discovery session before the engagement starts.

Retainer pricing is based on three factors: the tier (which sets the SLA, response guarantees, and feature scope), the size and complexity of the product being supported (number of services, infrastructure components, integrations), and the volume of maintenance hours allocated per month. We quote after a brief product assessment — usually a one-hour call and a codebase review — so the quote reflects what your product actually needs rather than a generic bracket. Minimum engagement is three months; most ongoing relationships run on rolling 12-month terms with a 90-day exit notice.
