Product Engineering — Maintenance

Your software does not
maintain itself

Every production application accumulates technical debt, security exposure, and dependency risk over time. Without structured maintenance, the cost of a security breach, an extended outage, or an emergency rebuild will far exceed the cost of the maintenance that prevented it. We provide retainer-based maintenance that keeps your systems healthy, your team focused, and your risks known.

Why It Matters

What deferred maintenance looks like in practice

Technical debt accumulates quietly. The signs are rarely obvious until the system fails. These are the patterns we see most often in codebases that arrive at us after years without structured maintenance.

⚠
Node packages or Python libraries that are years behind current releases and carrying known CVEs
⚠
Database queries that were fast with 10,000 records and unusably slow with 500,000
⚠
Third-party API integrations that were never updated when the provider changed their endpoints
⚠
SSL certificates that expired overnight, taking the application offline until someone noticed
⚠
No staging environment, so every fix goes directly to production with no way to test first
6 months unmaintained

Dependency drift

Key libraries fall two or three major versions behind. Security advisories start accumulating. The upgrade path that would have taken two hours now requires a week.

12 months unmaintained

Security exposure

Multiple critical CVEs in unmaintained packages. The application becomes a viable target. A breach at this stage typically costs more than three years of maintenance would have.

18 months unmaintained

Performance degradation

Query times have grown. Memory leaks that were imperceptible at launch now cause weekly restarts. User experience has degraded silently for months.

24+ months unmaintained

Partial rebuild required

Some dependencies are now incompatible with each other. The upgrade path no longer exists without replacing substantial portions of the application.

Scope of Work

What structured maintenance covers

Maintenance is not just bug fixing. These are the categories of work included in a Professional or Enterprise retainer, regardless of which issues surface in a given month.

🔒

Security and dependency management

Monthly audit of all third-party packages and libraries, CVE monitoring, and timely application of security patches to close known vulnerabilities.

📈

Performance monitoring and tuning

Continuous monitoring of response times, error rates, and resource consumption. Database query optimisation and infrastructure right-sizing when profiles indicate waste or degradation.

🛠

Bug investigation and resolution

Structured bug triage, root cause analysis, and resolution within SLA response times. Each fix is documented with cause, resolution, and prevention measure.

📄

Monthly technical health reporting

Written report covering system health indicators, work completed in the period, upcoming risks identified, and recommendations for the next period.

Common Questions

Before we start

A maintenance retainer covers the ongoing work needed to keep a production application healthy. This includes bug investigation and resolution, security patch application, dependency updates, performance monitoring and incident response, minor feature adjustments within agreed scope, and monthly technical review reports. The scope is defined in a Service Level Agreement before the engagement begins, so there is no ambiguity about what is covered.

Emergency incidents are handled under the response time defined in the client's SLA tier. For critical production outages — systems that are completely unavailable or losing data — our standard critical response target is two hours from report to first engineer on the problem. We triage the issue, implement a fix or rollback, and provide a written incident report within 24 hours covering root cause, resolution steps, and measures taken to prevent recurrence.

Yes. We start with a codebase audit — a structured assessment of the application's architecture, dependency status, security posture, test coverage, and documentation quality. This gives us a clear picture of the technical health of the system before we take responsibility for it. The audit typically takes five to ten business days depending on the complexity of the codebase, and it produces a written health report that we review with the client before the maintenance engagement begins.

Maintenance is technical work on the software itself — fixing bugs, applying patches, updating libraries, improving performance. Support is assistance provided to users of the software — answering questions, resolving account or data issues, handling requests. The two are often bundled together but they are distinct. Our Software App Maintenance service focuses on the technical health of the application. Our Product Maintenance and Support service covers both user-facing support operations and technical maintenance together.

Maintenance retainers are priced on a monthly basis based on the complexity of the application, the response time SLAs required, and the volume of maintenance work expected per month. We offer three tiers — Essential, Professional, and Enterprise — each with defined included hours, response times, and reporting. The monthly fee is predictable: clients know what maintenance costs before it happens, rather than receiving variable invoices for ad hoc work.
