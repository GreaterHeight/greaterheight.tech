AI strategy that starts with your business, not the technology

We help organisations cut through AI hype and identify where artificial intelligence creates genuine business value. From readiness assessments to implementation roadmaps, our consulting practice connects technology decisions to the outcomes your operation actually needs.

Our Engagement Model

Three phases. One connected outcome.

Every AI consulting engagement moves through three structured phases designed to give leadership teams a clear view of where they are, where they should go, and exactly how to get there.

01
Readiness Assessment
2 to 3 weeks

We audit your data infrastructure, operational processes, people capability, and regulatory environment to produce a scored AI readiness profile. This is not a theoretical exercise. We examine the actual systems you run, the data you generate, and the decisions your teams make daily.

02
Strategy Workshops
1 to 2 weeks

1 to 2 weeks

Who We Work With

AI consulting is not for every organisation at every moment

It is for organisations that have reached a decision point: where the pressure to adopt AI is real, the internal clarity about how to do so is not, and the cost of a wrong move is significant.

Technology Leadership
The CTO building a credible AI plan

You understand the technology. What you need is a structured argument for the board about where to invest, in what sequence, and what governance structure ensures the investment does not create liability. You need a document that holds up to scrutiny, not a deck full of vendor promises.

Needs to justify AI spend to non-technical board members
Lacks internal bandwidth to run a rigorous evaluation process
Wants an independent perspective before committing to a platform
Business Leadership
The Operations Director under pressure to modernise

Your processes are not broken, but they are slower and more expensive than they should be. The board is asking about AI. You need someone to identify specifically which processes are realistic AI candidates, what the implementation actually involves, and what could go wrong.

Receiving AI pitches from vendors with no independent evaluation
Not sure which operational problems AI actually solves
Concerned about disruption risk during implementation
Growth Leadership
The founder scaling with AI before competitors do

You are building at speed and AI is part of your product vision. You need to know which AI investments belong in the core product and which can be bought off-shelf, how to build an AI-native engineering culture, and where the regulatory risks sit in your target markets.

AI strategy is being shaped by what the engineering team prefers, not what the business needs
Investors asking about AI strategy without internal clarity on the answer
Needs to move fast without accumulating technical and compliance debt
Common Questions

What to expect before you engage us

An AI readiness assessment examines four dimensions of your organisation: data infrastructure (what data you have, its quality, and how it flows), process landscape (which operational processes are candidates for AI augmentation), people and skills (what internal capability exists and what needs to be built or hired), and risk and compliance posture (what regulatory and ethical constraints govern your AI use). The output is a scored readiness profile and a prioritised list of AI initiatives ranked by business value and implementation feasibility. Engagements typically take two to three weeks and include a structured presentation to leadership.

General technology consulting tends to focus on systems, integrations, and delivery. AI consulting deals with a category of technology that behaves probabilistically rather than deterministically, meaning the outputs vary, the system learns, and the failure modes are qualitatively different from conventional software. Good AI consulting requires depth in the specific domain of machine learning, language models, and agent systems, as well as familiarity with business operations. We bridge those two worlds: our consultants have shipped production AI systems, not just advised on them.

Yes. Vendor evaluation is a core part of our strategy engagements. We produce structured evaluation frameworks that assess vendors across capability fit, pricing models, data handling and privacy practices, integration complexity, and long-term viability. We have no commercial relationships with AI vendors, which means our recommendations are based entirely on fit for your use case. We cover foundation model providers, AI application platforms, vector database vendors, MLOps tooling, and domain-specific AI solutions relevant to your industry.

An AI implementation roadmap is a sequenced plan for deploying AI initiatives across your organisation over a 12 to 24 month horizon. It names each initiative, its business objective, the data and infrastructure prerequisites, the team and skills required, the delivery timeline, and the success metrics that determine whether the initiative has worked. It also includes a governance section covering who approves AI decisions, how models are monitored, and what the escalation path is when something goes wrong. The roadmap is a decision document, not a Gantt chart: it is designed to be revised as you learn.

Yes. GreaterHeight's consulting practice is backed by our engineering teams who build and deploy AI systems in production. This means our recommendations are grounded in what is actually buildable and what breaks in real deployments. Many clients begin with a consulting engagement to establish strategy and then continue with us into the engineering phase. The continuity eliminates the translation cost between strategy and implementation.
