AI & Automation

AI systems degrade silently. Yours will not.

Production Assurance is the operational discipline that keeps AI systems performing, governed, and trustworthy after they go live. It is not an add-on. It is what turns a successful launch into a durable capability that your business can rely on.

What Goes Wrong

The three risk categories that cause AI systems to fail in production

AI failures in production are rarely dramatic. They are gradual and invisible until they produce a consequential outcome. Understanding the risk categories is the first step toward managing them.

High risk
Output Quality Degradation

The AI produces outputs that are factually inaccurate, contextually inappropriate, or structurally malformed. This category includes hallucinations, outdated information, and responses that fail to follow the instructions the system was designed to enforce. These failures directly affect users and may have legal, financial, or reputational consequences for the organisation.

Hallucinations increasing over time as model updates are applied by provider
Factual information in the system prompt becoming outdated
Output format deviating from specification as edge cases are encountered
Medium risk
Distribution and Behaviour Drift

The system was designed and evaluated for a specific range of inputs. As usage expands, the actual distribution of inputs shifts. New user segments bring different vocabulary, intent patterns, and task complexity. The system behaves well for the original user base and poorly for the new one, a discrepancy that is invisible without monitoring.

New user segments with different query patterns entering the system
Seasonal or event-driven shifts in topic distribution
Model provider update changing output style without changing declared version
Operational risk
Cost and Performance Anomalies

AI systems incur costs proportional to usage. Without monitoring, cost spikes from unexpected usage patterns, prompt injection attacks that inflate token consumption, or configuration errors that cause excessive retries go undetected until they appear on a monthly invoice. Latency degradation under load may also go unreported if users tolerate it without escalating.

Prompt injection causing inflated token usage
Latency regression after model or infrastructure change
Cost growth from unexpected usage patterns or misconfigured retry logic
The Programme

Five pillars of Production Assurance

Each pillar addresses a distinct failure mode. Together they form a coherent programme that makes AI system performance visible, controllable, and auditable.

01
Evaluate
Continuous Automated Evaluation

We maintain a curated set of test cases derived from your production use case. An automated evaluation harness runs these cases against the live system on a defined schedule, scoring outputs against criteria relevant to your business: factual accuracy, instruction following, output format compliance, and task completion. When scores drop below threshold on any category, the monitoring system raises an alert before users experience the degradation.

Monitor
Performance and Cost Monitoring

We instrument your AI system with dashboards that track latency percentiles, token consumption, error rates, and cost per request. Anomaly detection identifies statistical outliers from baseline — a latency spike, an unexpected cost increase, or a sudden change in error rate — and triggers notifications to your team and to us. All metrics are retained with sufficient history to identify trends, not just point-in-time anomalies.

Respond
Incident Response and Remediation

When an alert fires, we have a defined response process: triage within a committed timeframe, root cause identification, remediation recommendation, and either a hot fix or a planned maintenance window depending on severity. We maintain a version-controlled history of your system prompt and configuration so that rollback to a known-good state is always available as an immediate mitigation, regardless of the root cause.

Governance and Audit Reporting

Every quarter we produce a governance report that documents system performance, test results, any incidents that occurred and how they were resolved, configuration changes made and why, and recommendations for the next period. The report is designed to satisfy internal governance requirements and, for organisations in regulated industries, to serve as a component of the audit evidence required by applicable AI governance frameworks. All change history is maintained in an immutable log.

Each improvement is scoped, tested against the evaluation harness before deployment, and documented in the change log.

What We Instrument

The monitoring coverage your AI system needs to be trusted in production

Effective Production Assurance requires visibility across four distinct dimensions. A gap in any one of them creates a blind spot that can conceal a failure until it reaches users.

📈
Output Quality Tracking

Automated evaluation scoring on factual accuracy, instruction adherence, format compliance, and task completion. Trend lines show whether quality is stable, improving, or degrading over time.

⚙
System Performance Metrics

End-to-end latency across p50, p95, and p99 percentiles. API availability and error rate tracking. Throughput monitoring with baseline alerting for deviation beyond statistical norms.

💲
Cost and Token Analytics

Token consumption broken down by request type, user segment, and time period. Cost-per-interaction trending. Anomaly detection for unexpected consumption spikes that could indicate injection attacks or misuse.

🔒
Safety and Compliance Logging

Full audit trail of inputs, outputs, and intermediate reasoning for systems where compliance requires it. Human oversight queue with volume and resolution time tracking. Policy violation detection and alerting.

Common Questions

What teams want to understand before committing to Production Assurance

Unlike traditional software, AI systems do not fail in obvious ways. A rule-based system either executes its logic or throws an exception; an AI model degrades gradually and silently. The underlying language model may be updated by the provider, changing output behaviour without notice. The distribution of real-world inputs your system receives shifts away from your training and evaluation data as usage patterns evolve. Adversarial users find prompts that expose edge-case behaviours. Factual information referenced in your system prompt becomes outdated. These are not theoretical risks; they are the normal lifecycle of an AI system in production. Without a monitoring programme, you discover these issues from user complaints rather than from your own instruments.

Model drift is a change in the statistical properties of a model's outputs over time. It has two main forms. Data drift occurs when the inputs the system receives in production differ from the inputs it was designed and evaluated for — the vocabulary, the complexity, the topic mix, or the user intent all shift gradually as the product reaches new user segments. Concept drift occurs when the underlying relationship the model learned becomes less valid over time. We detect drift using an automated evaluation harness that runs a fixed set of reference test cases against the live system on a scheduled basis. When the pass rate on any test category drops below a threshold, we receive an alert and investigate.

A Production Assurance retainer covers five things: continuous automated evaluation (a scheduled evaluation suite that runs your system against a maintained set of test cases and flags regressions), performance monitoring (latency, cost, and availability dashboards with alerting), incident response (a defined SLA for investigation and remediation when an alert fires), governance reporting (a monthly report documenting system performance, test results, any incidents, and recommended changes), and prompt and configuration management (version-controlled management of your system prompt and configuration, with a change log and the ability to roll back). Retainers are scoped to the number of AI systems covered and the complexity of the evaluation suite, and are renewed quarterly.

Hallucinations are managed through a combination of system design and ongoing evaluation. At the system design level, we use retrieval-augmented generation to ground the model's answers in verifiable source documents, design output formats that make claims attributable to sources, and build confidence thresholds that route low-confidence outputs to human review rather than directly to users. At the evaluation level, we maintain a set of factual accuracy test cases that are run on a scheduled basis. When factual accuracy scores drop, we investigate whether the issue is in the retrieval layer, the prompt, or the model, and address accordingly. For regulated industries, we maintain an audit trail of citations connecting every AI-generated claim to the source document that supports it.

Yes. AI governance requirements vary by jurisdiction and industry. We help organisations map their AI use cases to applicable regulatory frameworks, document the controls they have in place, and produce the audit evidence those frameworks require. This includes maintaining records of model versions and configuration changes, documenting evaluation methodology and results, recording human oversight decisions, and producing risk assessments for AI systems that make or influence consequential decisions. For organisations in sectors with explicit AI-related obligations — financial services, healthcare, education — we build the governance programme against the specific requirements of those frameworks rather than a generic model.
