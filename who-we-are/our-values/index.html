Our Values

The standard we hold
ourselves to when
no one is watching.

Every organisation publishes values. Few make them operational. These are the six commitments that govern how GreaterHeight works — not as aspirations, but as the filter through which every decision, every hire, and every client relationship is evaluated.

Values are behaviour under pressure. Everything else is branding.

What makes a value real?

A value becomes real the moment it costs something. Any organisation can list words like "integrity" or "excellence" on a website. The question is: what happens when holding to that value is inconvenient? When the client is wrong, but pushing back risks the relationship? When the honest technical recommendation is the more expensive one — for the client? When a deadline can only be met by cutting corners on documentation, testing, or code quality?

The values below are the ones GreaterHeight has genuinely paid for over fifteen years of practice. They cost us in the short term. They compound in the long term. They are the reason clients return for second and third engagements, recommend us without being asked, and trust us with work that matters.

We describe each value, say what it demands in practice, and name the behaviour it produces — because a value without a behavioural signature is a hope, not a standard.

Our Six Core Values

01

Reliability

We do what we say we will do, by when we said we would do it.

The discipline of keeping commitments

Reliability is the base layer. Without it, every other value is theoretical. It means that when GreaterHeight gives a timeline, that timeline is built from honest capacity planning — not from what the client wants to hear. It means that when a delivery date slips, we say so early enough for the client to adjust, not the day before.

In practice, reliability means we do not overpromise scope to win a project. We do not make verbal commitments we have not calculated. We maintain transparent progress reporting so the client never has to chase us for a status update. And when something goes wrong — as it sometimes will in any complex technical engagement — we surface the problem promptly and with a recovery plan, not an excuse.

Fifteen years of repeat clients is the compound interest of reliability. Trust is built in small moments: the report delivered on time, the call returned, the milestone hit. It erodes in small moments too.

Honest scope estimates Early risk disclosure Proactive status updates Milestone accountability

02

Technical Depth

We build things properly, or we explain why the alternative is better.

Knowing enough to know what matters

There is a version of a software project where the output looks finished but the foundations are weak — shortcuts taken, edge cases ignored, security overlooked, documentation absent. That version passes the demo. It fails at scale. It accrues technical debt that will cost far more to fix than the time saved cutting corners.

GreaterHeight's commitment to technical depth means we build things the right way for the context — which is not always the most sophisticated way, but is always the most sustainable one. Our engineers are expected to understand why an architecture decision was made, not just to implement it. Our code reviews focus on correctness and maintainability, not just style. Our documentation is written for the developers who will inherit the system, not for the record.

We invest in continuous learning because technology moves fast, and a firm that stopped learning in 2020 is giving 2020 advice in a 2026 market. Technical depth is a commitment to staying current, staying curious, and staying honest when we have reached the edge of our expertise.

Proper architecture decisions Honest expertise limits Continuous learning culture Sustainable code quality

03

Client Partnership

We are invested in their success, not just the success of the project.

The difference between a vendor and a partner

A vendor delivers what was ordered. A partner thinks about whether what was ordered is actually what is needed. The distinction matters most when the client has made a decision that, in our professional judgement, is not the right one. A vendor executes. A partner says so — clearly, with reasoning, and with an alternative recommendation.

Client partnership means we read the brief critically. We ask questions before we start building. We challenge assumptions when the evidence warrants it. And when the client's vision and the technical reality are in conflict, we broker that conversation early enough to matter.

It also means we celebrate their wins as our wins. When a platform we built contributes to a client's business growth, that outcome is not just their achievement — it is evidence that the partnership delivered on its promise. We track client outcomes beyond delivery because we are invested in the result, not just the invoice.

Critical brief reading Honest recommendations Post-delivery interest Outcome tracking

04

Contextual Intelligence

The best solution is the one that works in the environment it will actually live in.

Building for the real world, not the ideal one

Technology does not operate in a vacuum. A data pipeline designed for reliable high-speed internet may perform poorly in a market where connectivity is intermittent. A mobile application built for the latest flagship hardware may be unusable on the mid-range devices that dominate a particular market. An enterprise platform optimised for a team of fifty engineers may be unmaintainable for a team of five.

Contextual intelligence means we invest time in understanding the actual operating environment before we design a solution. That includes the technical context — infrastructure, connectivity, device profiles, team capacity — and the business context: the regulatory environment, the competitive landscape, the timeline pressures, the risk appetite.

This is particularly important for African markets, where the gap between imported technology assumptions and local operating reality is often significant. Solutions designed for Silicon Valley conditions do not automatically translate to Lagos, Nairobi, or Accra. Building for context is not a limitation — it is precision engineering applied to the right problem.

Environment-first design Local market awareness Constraint-aware architecture Regulatory literacy

05

Knowledge Transfer

A client who cannot run what we built has not been well served.

Building capability, not dependency

The easiest business model in technology consulting is to make yourself indispensable by making the client dependent on you. Opaque systems. Undocumented decisions. Code only your team understands. The client cannot leave because they cannot function without you. Revenue is reliable. The relationship is captive.

GreaterHeight rejects this model entirely. Our goal is a client who, at the end of an engagement, owns what we built — who can operate it, extend it, hand it to another team if needed, and explain to their own stakeholders how it works and why it was built the way it was. That requires documentation that is written for the people who will use it, not the people who wrote the system. It requires structured handover processes. It requires training where training is needed.

The paradox is that clients who trust you enough to become capable choose to keep working with you by preference, not necessity. The client relationships we are most proud of are the ones where the client could leave — and stays because the work is good.

Thorough documentation Structured handover Client team training Transparent architecture

06

African Excellence

Every engagement is a statement about what African technology firms are capable of.

The standard we carry on behalf of a continent

GreaterHeight was founded in Lagos. It will always be an African firm, regardless of where its offices expand. That origin is not a qualifier — it is a source of obligation. When we deliver work that is rigorous, well-documented, on time, and technically excellent, we are not only serving a client. We are building a case for the credibility of African technology in global markets.

This is not a burden we carry reluctantly. It is a standard we set for ourselves because we believe African technology firms are capable of competing with the best in the world — and we intend to prove it repeatedly, one engagement at a time. We do not accept lower standards because others might expect them. We set the highest standard we know how to set, and we hold ourselves to it.

African excellence also means telling African stories in technology — building solutions to African problems with an understanding of African contexts that no offshore firm can replicate. That knowledge is a competitive advantage and a responsibility.

Global standard, local context African market expertise Sector credibility building Representation through quality

What This Looks Like

Values in the moment of decision

Abstract values become visible when there is pressure. Here is what our values look like when they are tested — and what they do not look like.

Not this

Telling a client their timeline is achievable when the capacity plan says it is not, because the alternative is an uncomfortable conversation.

Recommending a technology because it is fashionable or profitable for us, when a simpler solution would serve the client better.

Delivering a system that only our team understands, to protect future revenue through client dependency.

Applying a solution designed for a different market to an African context without adapting it to what will actually work here.

Waiting until a project is in trouble to tell the client, because early disclosure feels like admitting failure.

This instead

Honest timeline conversations at scoping — grounded in actual capacity, with enough buffer to absorb reality, and communicated clearly before the work begins.

Technology recommendations based on the client's situation — including the recommendation to use less technology, or to wait, or to solve the problem a different way.

Handovers designed for the client team — documentation that a new engineer can follow, training sessions that build genuine competence, and code that is readable by design.

Solutions calibrated to the actual operating environment — accounting for infrastructure constraints, team maturity, connectivity, and regulatory context before a line of code is written.

Early disclosure of risks and blockers — with a proposed path forward already drafted, so the conversation is about choosing an option rather than absorbing a shock.

In Practice

Where you see these values in the work

These values are not internal documents. They show up in client interactions, in technical decisions, and in how we handle situations that do not go to plan.

Scenario

A discovery engagement reveals that the brief is based on a flawed assumption

We say so — in writing, with our reasoning, and with a recommended redirect. The client may not want to hear it. We would rather have that conversation at discovery than at delivery.

Client Partnership · Reliability

Scenario

A client requests a technology stack that is not the right fit for their team's capability

We recommend the stack their team can actually own and operate — and explain the cost, in time and money, of maintaining a system built on technology they cannot support internally.

Technical Depth · Knowledge Transfer

Scenario

An external dependency delays a milestone and the client has not been told

We notify the client the day we identify the risk — not the day the milestone is missed. We bring a revised schedule and a mitigation plan to that conversation, not just a problem.

Reliability · Client Partnership

Scenario

A solution designed for the EU market is proposed for deployment in West Africa

We assess the operating environment first. Infrastructure assumptions, connectivity realities, device profiles, and regulatory requirements are reviewed before a line of code is written.

Contextual Intelligence · African Excellence

Scenario

The handover documentation is minimal and the client team is not confident with the system

We do not consider the engagement complete until the client team can operate the system independently. Documentation and training are delivery requirements, not optional extras.

Knowledge Transfer · Client Partnership

Scenario

An international client questions whether an African firm can meet their standards

We answer with the quality of the work, the rigour of our process, and the track record of fifteen years of delivered projects. Then we get on with proving it.

African Excellence · Technical Depth
